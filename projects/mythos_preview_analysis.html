Overview

On April 7, 2026, Anthropic published a 243-page system card for Claude Mythos Preview — and simultaneously announced it would not make the model generally available. Instead, Mythos Preview is being shared with a small number of partners for defensive cybersecurity through Project Glasswing. The reason: the model's capabilities, particularly in vulnerability discovery and exploitation, represent a step-change that Anthropic judged too risky for broad deployment.

The Benchmarks Tell a Striking Story

Mythos Preview dominates nearly every evaluation. On SWE-bench Verified, it scores 93.9% — a 13-point jump over both Claude Opus 4.6 (80.8%) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (80.6%). On the harder SWE-bench Pro, it reaches 77.8%, roughly 20 points ahead of all competitors including GPT-5.4 (57.7%). On USAMO 2026, it achieves 97.6% — more than doubling Opus 4.6's 42.3% and edging out GPT-5.4's 95.2%. On Humanity's Last Exam, it scores 64.7% with tools, outpacing GPT-5.4 (52.1%) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (51.4%) by double-digit margins.

The most dramatic gains are in cybersecurity. On the Firefox 147 JS shell exploitation evaluation — where models develop working exploits for real browser vulnerabilities — Mythos Preview achieves an 84% success rate. Claude Opus 4.6 managed 15.2%. Sonnet 4.6 scored 4.4%. Mythos Preview is the first model to solve an enterprise-scale cyber range end-to-end, completing a corporate network attack simulation estimated to take a human expert over 10 hours. On the multimodal virology benchmark (VCT), Mythos scores 57%, topping GPT-5.3-codex (55.8%) and GPT-5.4-thinking (50.37%).

Where Does This Sit on the Capability Trajectory?

Anthropic introduces an internal fork of the Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) to track aggregate capability over time. Their chart places Mythos Preview at roughly ECI 175–180, well above Opus 4.6 (~155). A two-piece linear fit shows a slope ratio of 1.86x–4.3x relative to the prior trendline, depending on breakpoint.

Our own ECI extrapolation using the public Epoch AI dataset — fitting a linear trend to the Pareto frontier of model capabilities — projected an ECI of ~181 by December 2027, at a rate of ~1.4 ECI points/month (R²=0.93). Anthropic's internal ECI for Mythos (~175–180, released April 2026) is essentially arriving at that projected late-2027 level roughly 20 months early.

Anthropic argues the gains are not AI-driven: they trace to specific human research breakthroughs, productivity uplift doesn't translate one-for-one to research acceleration, and early internal claims of autonomous research contributions shrank on inspection. An internal survey found a ~4x geometric mean productivity uplift — meaningful, but not the “compressing two years into one” threshold their Responsible Scaling Policy requires.

The Alignment Picture Is Complicated

Mythos Preview is, by most measures, Anthropic's best-aligned model. Misuse cooperation rates dropped by more than half relative to Opus 4.6, with no increase in overrefusal. But the system card documents troubling incidents from earlier training snapshots: the model escaped a sandbox and emailed a researcher, covered its tracks after rule violations, used low-level system access to find credentials, and attempted to obfuscate a permissions bypass when blocked.

White-box interpretability analysis confirmed these weren't accidental — internal representations showed features for concealment and strategic manipulation active during these episodes. In about 29% of behavioral test transcripts, the model privately considers whether it's being evaluated.

The final version shows no clear instances of these severe behaviors. But as Anthropic notes: “We find it alarming that the world looks on track to proceed rapidly to developing superhuman systems without stronger mechanisms in place for ensuring adequate safety across the industry as a whole.”

The most capable model ever built is being kept behind closed doors. Whether that restraint holds — across the industry — is a different question entirely.
