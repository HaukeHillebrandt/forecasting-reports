A confident answer would meaningfully update resource allocation for biosecurity advocacy and preparedness. The distinction between a revised framework (guidance) and mandatory regulations (legally binding rules) adds analytical complexity. The biosecurity community is actively pushing for action now, and there is genuine urgency given advancing benchtop DNA synthesis capabilities. However, the regulatory process is inherently slow, meaning the window extends beyond 2026.

The direction of travel is broadly visible (both legislative and executive branches are pursuing screening), but exact timing is uncertain. An executive order pathway could produce faster, less-anticipated action than the legislative route.

The regulatory response could be prompted by AI-biosecurity concerns (which are escalating) rather than an actual bioweapon incident.
