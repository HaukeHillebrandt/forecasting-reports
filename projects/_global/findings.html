Prompts v2 prompts eliminated benchmark over-indexing

Your suspicion was right and quantified: the old prompts over-produced "will model X hit Y% on benchmark Z" questions instead of decision-relevant real-world outcomes, and the quality filter made it worse (42% → 44% of finals). The v2 generation prompt added a hard 1-in-4 benchmark cap, archetype quotas, and de-benchmark reframing.

PromptsProto-questionsBenchmark-flavored
v1 (6 papers)14822%
v2 (2 papers)300%

The 0% held even on the Lyptus paper — which is itself about cyber-capability benchmarks, the maximum-temptation case. v2 instead produced incident, legislation, treaty, and agency-publication questions.

Caveat: v2 sample is 30 protos over 2 papers — directional, not a controlled trial. The cleanest test (same paper under both prompt sets) costs ~$25/paper and the everyrow budget is spent.

Experiment Deep research materially improves forecasts (not noise)

We fed a Gemini deep-research-max report into the Fable second-opinion stage and measured the forecast against the claude-CLI's own sampling spread. On the US-federal-equity question, 3 runs each way:

ArmRunsMeanRange
No deep research25, 32, 3029.025–32
With deep research42, 37, 3738.737–42

The ranges do not overlap (DR min 37 > no-DR max 32), so the ~+10pp lift is a real effect, beyond the ~7pp sampling band. Consistent with the blind experiment: research-backed questions beat memory-only ones. The reports were substantive — the cyber one surfaced Anthropic's "Project Glasswing"; the equity one built a federal-precedent ladder (Intel 9.9% → mining → quantum → AI).

Caveats: one cleanly-controlled question (n=1); 3 deep-research-max calls cost ~$12–21 (5.6M tokens, 0 cached); the report itself can be wrong (no ground truth here).

Experiment Blinded: the pipeline beats a one-shot model

The high-effort pipeline produced one final question; Fable 5 wrote five in a single pass with no web access (locked before the pipeline output existed). Three blinded judges scored all six, shuffled.

OverallTournament-acceptJudges guessing "pipeline"
Pipeline question80.03/33/3
One-shot mean (5 Qs)60.611/150/15

The pipeline question won every judge's #1 slot and beat even the best one-shot (74.3). The mechanism is web research: two one-shot questions failed on factual currency (one already-determined, one mooted by a Dec-2025 act) — exactly the class the pipeline's research stages catch. Judges went 18/18 guessing the source — pipeline output is stylistically unmistakable.

Caveats: n=1 pipeline question; the one-shot had no web access by design; generator/judges are all Claude-family (possible taste correlation).

Architecture Most pipeline stages never bind — and the costly ones add least

An evidence audit across all stored projects found the 14-stage pipeline is mostly inert as a filter, while its most expensive stages are its lowest-value:

  • Stage 04 (triple verification) dropped 1 question in 17; stage 02d removed 0 duplicates ever; stage 02c advanced every input in 5/7 projects; stage 05 killed 0/16 — but substantively rewrote 10/16 (it's an editor, not a gate).
  • The 06b/06c/06d "inflate-then-correct" chain moves forecasts (06b +8.7pp) then walks them back (06d −12.4pp) — and 06b alone is 63% of per-paper cost ($15.62 at low effort).
  • Four overlapping judges re-score the same three properties 4–6× each (the fragile-source rule appears in six prompts).

Recommendation: a 5-pass "consolidated" design (~0.45× cost) that gates or removes the 06b/c/d chain — validate with a one-paper blind A/B.
