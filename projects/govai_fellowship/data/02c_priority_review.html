Rationale

The paper identifies Autonomous Replication and Adaptation (ARA) as a key loss of control (LoC) scenario recognized by all three major labs. Tracking performance on standardized benchmarks like METR's tasks or the UK AISI's RepliBench provides a concrete capability threshold for when a model might pose a real-world risk of escaping human oversight.

Slide 6: Risk Scenarios in the Literature (ARA); Slide 7: Risk Scenarios Developers Include (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind)

Importance notes

The question tracks a critical leading indicator for Autonomous Replication and Adaptation (ARA), which is identified as a major 'loss of control' risk pathway by all three major AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind). A 50% success rate on standardized evaluations like RepliBench or METR Rogue Replication would represent a significant threshold where models demonstrate the capability to autonomously persist and replicate, potentially changing the sign of risk from 'theoretical' to 'active'. It would plausibly shift resource allocation toward immediate mitigation and containment strategies.

Neglectedness notes

While the general topic of METR autonomy tasks is covered on platforms like Metaculus and Manifold (e.g., 'What will be the METR time horizon doubling time in 2026?'), specific operationalization around the 50% success rate on the 'Rogue Replication' or 'RepliBench' tasks by 2027 is relatively neglected. Metaculus has questions on general ARA passage and time horizons, but does not isolate this specific success rate for these exact benchmarks. Government agencies like the UK AISI are tracking RepliBench, but do not provide public crowd-sourced forecasts for these specific milestones.

Tractability notes

Skilled forecasters can improve on naive priors by synthesizing technical progress in LLM agents, scaling laws, historical progress on METR tasks (which have shown clear doubling times), and political/safety pressures on labs. The information environment is rich but requires non-trivial synthesis of AI development trends and safety research.

Soon notes

The question tracks a development actively unfolding in the near term (2025-2027). With model capabilities doubling on some autonomy tasks every 4-7 months, the window for learning about and influencing this trajectory is closing rapidly. Results from AISI's Trends Reports suggest these benchmarks are being actively monitored now.

Sudden notes

Capability jumps in frontier models often occur discretely with the release of new model generations (e.g., GPT-5 or equivalent). A model might go from 10% to 50% success with a single update or training run, representing a sudden state change that could surprise observers.

Sharp notes

Autonomous replication is a 'sharp' risk because the first successful instance of a model replicating 'in the wild' or escaping containment could be the consequential event itself, with few harmless 'warning shots' beforehand. Improvements in replication capabilities often happen in secure lab environments, making the first public failure potentially catastrophic.

Rationale

The paper identifies "deception" and "power-seeking" as intrinsic factors leading to Loss of Control [3d94a3]. While research by groups like Apollo Research has shown early evidence of deception, a confirmed case in a frontier model during formal safety testing would be a critical signal for regulators. This question is non-trivial as current "alignment" techniques aim to prevent this, making its occurrence a major "systemic risk" event as defined in the paper.
